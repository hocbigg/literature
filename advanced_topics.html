Advanced Topics

Specialized subfields, advanced theoretical frameworks, and research methodologies for post-core literary studies. Learners are expected to choose one or two tracks aligned with their interests and critical goals rather than attempting to complete every track.

  • Critical Theory, Biopolitics & Affect: Investigates post-structuralist philosophies of textuality, the modern regulation of biological life and sovereign violence, bodily affect, and non-human agency beyond anthropocentric models.
  • Postcolonial, Decolonial & Transnational Studies: Examines imperial archives, subaltern agency, decolonial border thinking, and the uneven geopolitics of literary production and circulation across the Global South.
  • Gender, Sexuality & Queer Poetics: Explores the discursive construction of gender performativity, queer temporalities and spatialities, intersectional Black feminist thought, and transgender phenomenology.
  • Environmental Humanities & Ecocriticism: Analyzes literary representations of the non-human world, climate crisis, environmental justice, slow violence, and petrocultures in the Anthropocene.
  • Computational Literary Studies & Digital Humanities: Integrates quantitative distant reading, algorithmic text mining, corpus stylistics, and digital scholarly editing to analyze literary history at scale.
  • Advanced Narratology, Poetics & Experimental Forms: Scrutinizes cognitive and unnatural narrative architectures, high modernist formal experimentation, postmodern metafiction, and the historical evolution of literary genres.

Critical Theory, Biopolitics & Affect

Investigates post-structuralist philosophies of textuality, the modern regulation of biological life and sovereign violence, bodily affect, and non-human agency beyond anthropocentric models.

Post-Structuralism, Deconstruction and Textuality: Of Grammatology (Jacques Derrida, trans. Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak) - The foundational monograph of deconstruction, dismantling structuralist linguistics by demonstrating how writing precedes and destabilizes speech through différance, trace, and the supplement.

Biopolitics, Sovereignty and Disciplinary Power: The History of Sexuality, Volume 1: An Introduction (Michel Foucault) - The seminal text articulating the historical shift from sovereign violence to disciplinary power and the biopolitical management of living bodies and populations.

Biopolitics, Sovereignty and Disciplinary Power: Foucault 13/13 Seminar Series (Columbia Center for Contemporary Critical Thought / Bernard E. Harcourt) - An open university video seminar series that pairs Foucault's thirteen Collège de France lecture courses with in-depth critical commentary by leading contemporary theorists; use alongside Foucault's primary texts.

Affect Theory and Somatic Registers: Cruel Optimism (Lauren Berlant) - A transformative work that analyzes how contemporary cultural narratives, bodily sensations, and pre-reflective affective states register the slow systemic breakdown of late capitalism.

Posthumanism and New Materialisms: Vibrant Matter: A Political Ecology of Things (Jane Bennett) - Reconfigures critical reading by attributing agency and vital force to inorganic materials and non-human assemblages, overturning anthropocentric assumptions in textual analysis.

Postcolonial, Decolonial & Transnational Studies

Examines imperial archives, subaltern agency, decolonial border thinking, and the uneven geopolitics of literary production and circulation across the Global South.

Postcolonial Theory and Colonial Discourse: Postcolonial Literature (NPTEL / Sayan Chattopadhyay) - A comprehensive 20-lecture video course examining colonial discourse analysis, Commonwealth literature, Achebe, Rushdie, Said, and Bhabha.

Postcolonial Theory and Colonial Discourse: The Wretched of the Earth (Frantz Fanon) - The seminal anti-colonial text that analyzes the psychological, cultural, and political mechanisms through which colonized populations resist imperial subjugation; read in conjunction with the NPTEL lectures.

Subaltern Studies and Archival Silence: Provincializing Europe: Postcolonial Thought and Historical Difference (Dipesh Chakrabarty) - Interrogates the universalist claims of Western historiography and political theory, demonstrating how postcolonial narratives confront the limits of Eurocentric translation.

Decoloniality and Linguistic Delinking: Decolonising the Mind: The Politics of Language in African Literature (Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o) - The foundational manifesto on the cultural and political imperatives of writing in African mother tongues rather than the languages of imperial conquest.

World-Systems Literature and Combined Development: Combined and Uneven Development: Towards a New Theory of World-Literature (Warwick Research Collective) - Employs Wallerstein’s world-systems theory and Trotsky’s uneven development to demonstrate how peripheral and semi-peripheral economies generate distinct, non-linear literary forms.

Gender, Sexuality & Queer Poetics

Explores the discursive construction of gender performativity, queer temporalities and spatialities, intersectional Black feminist thought, and transgender phenomenology.

Gender Performativity and Subversive Identity: Gender Trouble: Feminism and the Subversion of Identity (Judith Butler) - The groundbreaking text establishing that gender is not an internal essence but an iterative performance enforced by compulsory heterosexuality and discursive power.

Intersectional Poetics and Black Feminist Thought: Sister Outsider: Essays and Speeches (Audre Lorde) - The canonical collection weaving poetic theory, personal essay, and radical politics to show how race, class, sexuality, and age operate as interlocking systems of difference.

Queer Temporalities and Utopian Aesthetics: Cruising Utopia: The Then and There of Queer Futurity (José Esteban Muñoz) - Critiques pragmatic anti-relational and reproductive models of time by arguing that queer aesthetic works from poetry to performance offer glimpses of a transformative utopian future.

Queer Phenomenology and Spatial Orientations: Queer Phenomenology: Orientations, Objects, Others (Sara Ahmed) - Synthesizes phenomenology with queer critique to investigate how physical and social spaces orient bodies along heterosexual trajectories and how queer deviations construct new ways of dwelling.

Environmental Humanities & Ecocriticism

Analyzes literary representations of the non-human world, climate crisis, environmental justice, slow violence, and petrocultures in the Anthropocene.

Environmental Literary History and Pastoral Geographies: English 22: Introduction to Literature and the Environment (Ken Hiltner / UC Santa Barbara) - A complete 16-lecture open university course tracing 5,000 years of literary history from Gilgamesh to the present to uncover how cultural texts shape our ecological attitudes.

Material Ecocriticism and Dark Ecology: Ecology Without Nature: Rethinking Environmental Aesthetics (Timothy Morton) - A provocative philosophical critique demonstrating that the romanticized, external concept of "nature" actually prevents genuine ecological awareness, advocating instead for an immersive "dark ecology."

Climate Fiction and Planetary Scales: The Great Derangement: Climate Change and the Unthinkable (Amitav Ghosh) - Examines why the modern realist novel struggles to depict the non-linear, collective violence of global climate disruption, calling for radical reinventions of narrative imagination.

Slow Violence and Environmental Justice: Slow Violence and the Environmentalism of the Poor (Rob Nixon) - The defining environmental humanities study that analyzes how writer-activists in the Global South render visible the delayed, attritional, and invisible damage of toxic waste, deforestation, and climate breakdown.

Computational Literary Studies & Digital Humanities

Integrates quantitative distant reading, algorithmic text mining, corpus stylistics, and digital scholarly editing to analyze literary history at scale.

Distant Reading and Macroanalysis: Distant Reading (Franco Moretti) - The seminal essay collection articulating the methodology of using quantitative graphs, geographic maps, and evolutionary trees to analyze entire literary systems beyond the limitations of single-text close reading.

Distant Reading and Macroanalysis: Distant Horizons: Digital Evidence and Literary Change (Ted Underwood) - Provides mathematical and machine-learning frameworks to track long-term transformations in genre, gender representation, and poetic diction across centuries of digitized books; read alongside Moretti.

Cultural Analytics and Computational Text Analysis: Introduction to Cultural Analytics & Python (Melanie Walsh) - An open-access interactive online textbook that teaches Python programming, natural language processing, topic modeling, and named entity recognition tailored specifically for humanities texts.

Digital Humanities Research Methodologies: Introduction to Digital Humanities (Harvard Online / edX) - A structured online course introducing digital textual scholarship, optical character recognition, digital archives, and spatial visualization workflows across humanities disciplines.

Practical Corpus Workflows and Text Mining: Programming Historian Lessons (The Programming Historian) - A peer-reviewed, open-access repository of modular tutorials covering hands-on computational text analysis, corpus preparation, sentiment analysis, and web scraping for literary materials.

Digital Scholarly Editing and Text Encoding: Digital Scholarly Editing: Theories and Practices (Elena Pierazzo) - Provides the authoritative theoretical and practical framework for digital textual criticism, XML and Text Encoding Initiative (TEI) standards, and creating dynamic, machine-readable editions.

Advanced Narratology, Poetics & Experimental Forms

Scrutinizes cognitive and unnatural narrative architectures, high modernist formal experimentation, postmodern metafiction, and the historical evolution of literary genres.

Cognitive and Unnatural Narratology: Towards a 'Natural' Narratology (Monika Fludernik) - A foundational study integrating cognitive linguistics and narrative theory to demonstrate how experientiality, consciousness representation, and reader schema construction drive storytelling across oral and written forms.

Postmodernism and Metafiction in Late Capitalism: The American Novel Since 1945 (Open Yale Courses / Amy Hungerford) - A full 26-lecture university course examining postwar American fiction, self-reflexive narration, metafiction, historical trauma, and minimalism through close readings of Nabokov, Pynchon, Morrison, and DeLillo.

Postmodernism and Metafiction in Late Capitalism: Postmodernism, or, The Cultural Logic of Late Capitalism (Fredric Jameson) - The definitive theoretical critique tracing how postmodern aesthetic forms—pastiche, depthlessness, and schizophrenia—mirror the global spatial logic of late consumer capitalism; watch Hungerford's lectures alongside this text.

Avant-Garde Poetics and Syntactic Indeterminacy: The Poetics of Indeterminacy: Rimbaud to Cage (Marjorie Perloff) - Redraws twentieth-century modernist poetics by tracing an alternative avant-garde tradition characterized by anti-mimetic collage, non-linear syntax, and radical indeterminacy.

Historical Poetics and the Emergence of the Novel: The Origins of the English Novel, 1600–1740 (Michael McKeon) - A monumental dialectical history demonstrating how the novel emerged as a cultural technology designed to resolve simultaneous crises in epistemological truth and social virtue.
